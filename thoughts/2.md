Just read https://andrewkelley.me/post/renting-is-for-suckers.html.

I've been thinking about this problem since I read https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first.

It looks like there is a movement, originated from different places, independently, that local-first software is important. Users should own their software and own their data, just like they own their cars, chairs, and clothes.

Good software engineerers are awakening. We don't accept the cyberpunk future that people won't be able to do the basic thing without connecting to a server owned by big organizations.

Also, it's my prediction that GPUs will be cheaper in the next 5-10 years and the scaling law of transformer based LLMs will reach its limit. At that time, users will be able to run a powerful LLM locally on their machine. The cloud-only architecture of most LLM-agents are just a temporary solution.

OpenCut will be built on this prediction. It will be a local-first software. Even if it connects to the internet to perform some tasks, 90% of the tasks should be doable locally on the user's machine.

It's a long journey. I am alone at the moment. But I believe that more and more people will join this movement.
