So I have been thinking about this project for years. I don't recall exactly since when, I wantted to implement my own video editor. At least for the past 3 - 4 years, this idea kept popping back in my mind once a while.

At first I was intimidated by the complexity of a video editor.

But, the world needs a new video editor that is
1. delightful to use
2. not controlled by any single corporation
3. possibly open sourced

Why? I have been a YouTuber for 10 years. Not a huge one, but I always liked making videos. I have used Davinci Resolve, PR and several other old school industry standard video editors.

These big heavy editors always feel unpleasant to use. It always feels like the only reason that I use them is because I got no other choices. There aren't simply no other viable options (that are good enough). It feels like living in a world where programmers are forced to use Visual Studio or Eclipse, but for video editors.

This does not feel right at all.

CapCut came out as a good alternative a few years ago and I liked it a lot despite that its lack of multi-timeline editing (at first). But it was 10X easier to use, more intuitive and fit for social media video use cases.

It was great, until it's not.

From 1 - 2 years ago, CapCut started to add more and more vendor-lock-in features and TikTok platform specific features. I knew it! Of course! CapCut is from ByteDance. It has no intrinsic motivation to stay independent from the platform. It has no reason to become an independent video editor.

But, it's like the only actually useable editor newly made in the past 20 years.

How could this be? At first I thought, maybe it's because making a video editor is hard. Too hard that you need millions of dollars to hire a world class team.

Or, maybe it's simply because most programmers suck, close minded and ignorant? I am the post-2010 generation of programmers. When I started learn programming, it's all about mobile & web frameworks. My whole generation and later generations of CS students & study-learned programmers are, at large, ignorant of actually system engineering. We know nothing beyond of the artificial boundary of Web APIs and backend frameworks. We don't know how a computer actually boot up. We don't know how a pixel is actually drawn on the screen. We don't even know the difference between analog and digital signals.

The point is, we are the generations of programmers, who lost the craft & knowledge. We are captive breeded by the languages & frameworks of big teches.

Videos are so important. It's the media of the social media era. It is the vehicle of information and ideas.

If we really care about our mental well-being. We should, and rightly deserve a video editor that's independent from any social media platform, independent from any particular interest group.

Maybe it's not that hard?

5 years ago, I watched a video that Johnathan Blow posted back in 2014 in which he described a new language he had in mind. Andrew, the creator of Zig, watched that video and was inspired to create a new language called Zig, at least partially by it.

Let's also talk about Zed the code editor. Their team created the original electron framework back in Github. Electron made VS Code possible. VS Code made Cursor possible.

If we are only talking about money, of course Zed's team are stupid because they did not make money from their old work and Cursor team are smart because they made the most money out of other people's work.

But, is it really so? Cursor is not pushing the human knowledge forward. Coding Agent is a low economic moat thing. Creating a new GUI framework and building a code editor reimagined, is, however, pushing the possibility forward. They might end up making much less money. But they are more anti-fragile than cursor because they actually understand more about system engineering, by choose the hard way. By re-inventing lots of things in Rust, Zed just feels better than any Browser-based code editor.

These are stories proving that with enough will power, knowledge and execution, a single person or a few people can achieve the impossible.

Plus, I think it's much easier to creator a video editor than creating a programming language or a game engine.

I don't need to re-invent everything. I just need to use Zig to hook up ffmpeg & other GUI libraries.

But staying native is important. Having a monolithic program that users can 100% run on their local machine is important.

Maybe I just need 1000 hours to prove my point.

Maybe I will fail because I run out of money and have to work another corp job. Or because the future proves that I am not smart enough to make a quality video editor.

But I believe the journey is necessary.

P.S. I won't use Rust because Rust is too hard for my brain & soul.
